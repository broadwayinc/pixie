5. Remixes

Any signed-in user can remix a public pixie: Remix opens a copy in their studio, and when they share it, it links back to the pixie it came from and joins that pixie's remix family, which plays as an animation.

When you share a remix, the maker of the original pixie has rights to your remix. Specifically, you grant to the maker of the first pixie in the remix family, and to the maker of the pixie you remixed if that is someone else, a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free license to use, copy, change, display, share and make new works from your remix, for any purpose, including commercial use, without payment or credit. They may also sublicense these rights.

The maker of the first pixie also decides the order in which the frames of the family play, and may remove your remix from the family:

  • In a family that supports it, removing your remix unlinks it. It is not deleted: it stops being a frame of the family right away. To unlink it, pixie removes only your remix's link to the family, and marks it as unlinked. The next time you open pixie, pixie moves it from your remixes to your pixies and drops the "remix of" credit: it becomes your regular pixie, listed like the other pixies you post (on your page, in Recent and under its tags, at the date you first shared it), with its likes and comments. Until then it is under My remixes. Its picture, caption and tags stay as you shared them.
  • Removing a remix from a family never deletes it. In an older family that doesn't support unlinking, or if your remix can't be unlinked, it simply stays in the family.
  • When the maker of the first pixie removes one of their own remixes from their family, it is unlinked the same way and becomes their regular pixie right away. The first pixie of a family can't be removed from it.

You can still delete your own remixes at any time. The license above stays in effect after your remix is unlinked or deleted.

A remix shared as private does not join the family, and this section does not apply to it.
